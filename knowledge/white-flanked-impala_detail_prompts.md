### White-Flanked Impala-Specific Characteristics:
- **Color Pattern**: White coloration of belly extends aggressively upwards, covering flanks and ribcage, often meeting dark dorsal saddle directly - eliminates transitional tan zone found in common impala
- **Face**: Often shows increased white markings around eyes and muzzle, giving "softer" appearance
- **Metatarsal Gland**: Tuft of black hair covering scent gland above heel on hind legs - remains prominent, creating stark high-contrast visual marker against white legs (critical for field identification)
- **Horns**: Lyre-shaped (S-shaped), growing outwards, backwards, then curving upwards, heavily ridged (annulated) for bottom two-thirds, smooth and sharp at tips
- **Size**: Mature ram: 50-80 kg (110-175 lb), 75-92 cm (30-36 in) at shoulder
- **Behavior**: Stotting (pronking) - leaps up to 3 meters high, 10-12 meters long. Rutting - guttural roaring grunt, single-minded focus on herding and fighting
- **Origin**: Color morph resulting from expression of naturally occurring recessive alleles. In free-range wilderness, color anomalies appear sporadically but often naturally culled by predation due to lack of camouflage. Proliferation is phenomenon of enclosed game ranching system

### Genetic and Economic Context:
- **Genetic Origin**: Not separate subspecies or hybrid - color morph of Common Impala (Aepyceros melampus melampus)
- **Recessive Trait**: Naturally occurring recessive alleles. In free-range, often naturally culled by predation due to lack of camouflage
- **Economic Value**: Trophy fee ranges $2,000-$3,000 USD (vs $250-$600 for Common Impala) - 4-8x relative value factor
- **Reputation**: Known as "McDonald's of the Bush" due to ubiquity as prey species and characteristic black "M" marking on rump. Multi-dimensional challenge: wariness of herd animal, agility of master escaper, aesthetic appeal of rare color variant

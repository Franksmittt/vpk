- **Environment**: Wetland floodplains, shallow water, soft mud, reeds and papyrus, dry "islands"
- **Lighting**: Golden hour (4:30-6:00 PM or early morning), warm directional light (4500K-5500K)

### Lechwe-Specific Characteristics:
- **Posture**: Hindquarters noticeably higher than shoulders (sloping posture)
- **Hooves**: Elongated, narrow, capable of extreme splaying (snowshoe effect)
- **Coat**: Long, rough, greasy/waxy, chestnut-red with white underparts
- **Horns**: Lyre-shaped, sweeping backward then curving forward (45-92cm)
- **Behavior**: Semi-aquatic, feeds in shallow water, uses water for thermoregulation and defense
- **Habitat**: Obligate wetland species, requires permanent water, shallow floodplains, dry islands

### Subspecies Reference:
The prompts reference the **Red Lechwe (Kobus leche leche)** as the primary subspecies, which is the most widely distributed and recognized. This subspecies is characterized by:
- Vibrant chestnut-red coat
- Robust build
- Found in Okavango Delta, Kafue Flats, Caprivi Strip
- The "baseline" morphological reference for the species

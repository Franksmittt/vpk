### Dapple Impala-Specific Characteristics:
- **Color Pattern**: Dapple/mosaic pattern - irregular, marbled spots of dark brown, black, and cream scattered across flank, neck, and hindquarters, resembling coat of dappled horse or wild dog
- **Pattern Variation**: No two Dapple Impalas identical. Can range from "Midnight Dapple" (predominantly black with small light spots) to "Mosaic Dapple" (even distribution of tri-color spots)
- **Coat Texture**: Hair short, glossy, lies flat against skin - typical for antelope in tick-rich environments
- **Metatarsal Gland**: Tuft of black hair covering scent gland above heel on hind legs - remains black even in Dapple variant, serving as critical identification point
- **Horns**: Lyre-shaped (S-shaped), growing outwards, backwards, then hooking upwards, heavily ridged (annulated) for basal two-thirds, smooth and polished at tips
- **Size**: Mature ram: 53-76 kg (117-167 lb), 75-92 cm (30-36 in) at shoulder
- **Behavior**: Pronking - leaps up to 3 meters high when threatened. Rutting - guttural roaring grunt, single-minded focus on herding and fighting
- **Origin**: Color morph resulting from specific genetic mutations affecting pigmentation. Commercial history tied to Batiki Game Farm genetics in Limpopo province

### Genetic and Economic Context:
- **Genetic Origin**: Not separate subspecies - color morph of Common Impala (Aepyceros melampus melampus). Mutation disrupts uniform production of phaeomelanin (red pigment) and eumelanin (black pigment), resulting in mosaic pattern
- **Recessive Trait**: Recessive or partially recessive mode of inheritance. Animal must inherit two copies of mutant gene to visually express color. Animal with one copy is "split" or "carrier"
- **Batiki Lineage**: Breeding rams such as "Nhahla" and "Asante Sana" from Batiki bloodline became cornerstones of industry. Selected for intensity of coat pattern ("loud" dapples) and horn volume. Produces Dapple rams competing with common impala in trophy mass with horn lengths exceeding 25 inches
- **Economic Value**: Trophy fee ~$11,500 USD (vs $250-$600 for Common Impala) - 20-45x relative value factor. Breeding cost: High initial capital for stud rams (up to $20,000+ for top genetics like Batiki lines)
- **Reputation**: Known as "Africa's goat" for its toughness. High-stakes endeavor requiring precision in identification, tracking, and shot placement. Market is "Exclusive" - clients typically repeat hunters looking for "Diamond" of collection

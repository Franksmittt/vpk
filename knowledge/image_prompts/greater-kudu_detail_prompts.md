### Greater Kudu-Specific Characteristics:
- **Coat Color**: Base color ranges from reddish-fawn in females and young animals to deep, slate-grey in mature bulls (often referred to as "Blue Bulls" in hunting vernacular). Overlaid are 6-10 thin, vertical white stripes running down flanks - in chaotic visual field of bushveld thicket, these mimic vertical shafts of light filtering through branches, creating disruptive camouflage pattern
- **Spiral Horns**: Grow in wide, open spiral, diverging from head in V-formation. Mature set typically completes 2.5 to 3 full rotations. Tips should point outward and backward, indicating full turns have been completed. Deep curl (like wrapping around wine barrel) consumes more length than tight, corkscrew spiral. Old bulls often wear down tips exposing white bone core ("ivory tips") - highly prized sign of age and character
- **Size**: Mature Bull: 190-270 kg (420-600 lb), 140-160 cm (55-63 in) at shoulder. Mature Cow: 120-210 kg (260-460 lb), 100-125 cm (39-49 in) at shoulder. Second-tallest antelope in Africa, surpassed only by Eland
- **Behavior**: Freeze response - immobility as first line of defense, trusting grey coats and disruptive stripes to blend into background. Look back - after being startled, runs short distance then stops to look back. Flagging - curling tail upwards exposing white scut as visual beacon. Horn wrestling - locking spiraled horns and twisting during rut conflicts. Death lock - two fighting bulls becoming inextricably interlocked (tragic phenomenon). Vocalizations - deep, guttural bark ("Vooo-ah!") when startled, can be heard for over kilometer
- **Origin**: Flagship member of family Bovidae and tribe Tragelaphini - "twist-horned" antelopes that emerged during Miocene epoch. Scientific nomenclature Tragelaphus strepsiceros: Tragos (he-goat) and elaphos (deer) combine to describe animal with browsing tenacity of goat and athletic grace of deer, while strephis (twisting) and keras (horn) refer to spiraling headgear

### Genetic and Economic Context:
- **Taxonomic Classification**: Family Bovidae, Tribe Tragelaphini. Southern Greater Kudu (T. s. strepsiceros) - nominate subspecies and primary focus of Southern African game industry, characterized by significant size and classic slate-grey to tawny coloration found in bushveld
- **Trophy Standards**: Average: 120 cm (47 inches) measured along spiral. Trophy standard: 54 inches. "Holy Grail": 60 inches (achieved by fewer than 5% of wild bulls). World record: 72 inches (historically rare). Judging criteria: Helix depth (most critical factor), tip direction (outward and backward indicates maturity), ivory tips (sign of age and character)
- **Economic Value**: Central pillar of Southern African game industry. For game reserve owner, Kudu is vital indicator of habitat health and primary economic asset. Trophy fee: ~$3,500 USD. The pursuit of Kudu bull often highlight of safari
- **Reputation**: Known as "Grey Ghost" of African bushveld. Master of camouflage - ability to vanish into dappled light despite immense size is legendary. Definitive test for spoorsnyer. Hunt rewards patience, optics, and stealth rather than raw distance. Challenge: Kudu have mediocre sense of sight but possessing hearing and smell that are legendary. Swirling wind in valley is Kudu hunter's worst enemy
